Grade 10 Mathematics

📘 Chap1: Powers and Radicals

Course Content

  1. Definition of powers of a real number: For any real number \(a\) and integer \(n\): \[a^n = \underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot a \cdots a}_{n \text{ times}}\] If \(n=0\), then \(a^0 = 1\) (for \(a \neq 0\)).
  2. Powers with rational and irrational exponents: For \(a > 0\) and rational exponent \(\frac{p}{q}\): \[a^{\frac{p}{q}} = \sqrt[q]{a^p}\] For irrational exponents, definition extends by limits.
  3. Properties of powers: \[a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}, \quad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}, \quad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}, \quad (ab)^n = a^n b^n\]
  4. n-th root of a real number: \[\sqrt[n]{a} = b \quad \text{iff} \quad b^n = a\] Properties: \(\sqrt[n]{ab} = \sqrt[n]{a}\sqrt[n]{b}\), \(\sqrt[n]{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}}\).

Exercises

5 Solved Examples

Example 1: Simplify \(2^3\).
Answer: \(2^3 = 8\).
Example 2: Simplify \(9^{1/2}\).
Answer: \(9^{1/2} = 3\).
Example 3: Simplify \(27^{2/3}\).
Answer: \(27^{2/3} = (27^{1/3})^2 = 3^2 = 9\).
Example 4: Simplify \(\frac{a^5}{a^2}\).
Answer: \(a^{5-2} = a^3\).
Example 5: Simplify \((x^2)^3\).
Answer: \(x^{2\cdot3} = x^6\).
